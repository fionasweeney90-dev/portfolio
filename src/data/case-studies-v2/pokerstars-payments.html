Project 1

Transaction History

My first major project at PokerStars: redesigning how players filter and find their transactions.

Hands-on designSix weeksDesktop & mobileLaunched

01 · Context

Every transaction, in one endless list

Transaction History showed every payment in one continuous list, with no way to narrow it down. Finding a single deposit or withdrawal meant scrolling and scanning, and players often contacted support instead. It also had to work across regulated markets, including the EU, Italy and Romania, each with its own requirements.

  • Find it fast: help players locate individual or grouped transactions quickly.
  • Build trust: make transactions more visible and transparent.
  • Reduce support: fewer tickets, higher satisfaction.
Close

Before: every transaction in one continuous list.

02 · What we learned

Players needed a quicker way to narrow it down

I reviewed previous player research and feedback, benchmarked filtering across competitors and e-commerce sites, and tested early concepts to see which felt clearest and quickest to use.

That led to separate filter and sort designs for mobile and desktop, each tailored to the device. I prototyped and tested them in Figma, and players found the new filters clear, especially the icons and visual cues.

03 · Key decision

Dropdowns or a modal? We let players decide

Custom dropdowns

The case
My design. Filters apply automatically, so players see results straight away.

The case

What happened
I was open to both, so I tested them. Players preferred the custom filters for clarity and ease of use. I shared the results with past research and best practice, and despite some pushback, the team aligned on the custom design.

05 · Impact

Easier to find, fewer support tickets

  • Players found transactions faster, which helped reduce support tickets and improve satisfaction scores.
  • A more evidence-led team: UX, Product and Engineering grew closer, and testing and iteration became a normal part of how we design.

Back design decisions with data, while staying open to feedback from others.

The lesson I took

I’d involve Engineering in concept testing from the start, so the dropdown-vs-modal debate happened before development rather than during it.

Project 2

Payment Method Management

A clearer, more trusted way for players to manage how they pay. I set the direction and mentored the junior designer who led the day-to-day work.

Design direction & mentoringFour monthsDesktop & mobileNot launched

06 · Context

Removing a card meant calling support

Players couldn’t manage their own payment methods. Even simple tasks, like removing a card or checking a limit, meant contacting support. And teams didn’t share an understanding of what “management” should mean.

The goal: give players more control, reduce support dependency, and build trust through clear design that was also compliant and realistic to build.

07 · What we learned

Most players use one card, but want somewhere to manage it

We surveyed 247 players across the UK and US, reviewed past research and support data, and interviewed stakeholders. Confusion around verification and limited control over cards came up again and again.

70%only use one payment method
3%use more than three
51%want a dedicated section
39%expect it in the Cashier

“Make sense just to keep everything related to payments in the same area.”

Player, on the Cashier

“Lost debit card so had to input new details of my card and had to request to have the old details removed by support to be able to do so.”

Player, on editing or removing a card

08 · Key decisions

A small first release, and a designer who could own it

1

Start with three actions

Why
Teams had different ideas of “management”, and the first release needed to be achievable.
How
I facilitated workshops with Product, CX and Engineering. We agreed View, Verify and Set as Preferred first, with Edit and Delete planned for later.
2

Let the junior designer lead

Why
A great chance for them to grow, with the right support around them.
How
They led day-to-day design. I owned the UX direction, supported wireframing and prototyping, and ran regular design reviews.
3

Design for compliance from the start

Why
Payments are regulated, so masking and verification couldn’t be an afterthought.
How
Aligned early with UI and Engineering on accessibility, hierarchy, masking and verification.

Clear, trusted and simple

10 · Reflections

Leading through someone else’s hands

  • Workshops set the tone: starting with Product, Engineering and CX in the room made the project open, aligned and evidence-led from day one.
  • Owning the roadmap meant balancing team capacity, and knowing when to move fast and when to slow down for better decisions.
  • Mentoring and ways of working: when Product began going directly to the junior designer, I agreed clearer ways of working with the PM and coached the designer to challenge requests with evidence. It strengthened both the work and the team.

Next time, I’d agree ways of working with the PM at kickoff, including who requests what and where design decisions get made, rather than fixing it once decisions had already started bypassing design.
