01 · Context

Three brands, three different searches, none of them working well

Search is one of the main ways players find and launch games across Paddy Power, Betfair and Sky Vegas. But results were slow and often inaccurate, each brand behaved differently, and there was little help for players who didn’t already know what they wanted.

The opportunity wasn’t a new coat of paint. It was building reliable foundations that could serve different player needs and three brand identities.

02 · What we learned

Players either know what they want, or need help finding it

Auditing the live experience

I audited the live search and captured where it let players down. Search wasn’t instant. Results only appeared after three letters. And when searching for Big Bass 2, a game that exists, it didn’t appear in the results at all.

Starting with people, not solutions

I started conversations across Product, Development, Data, Content, Design and Commercial about how they use search in everyday life, and what a good search feels like. We’re all users of search, so these surfaced assumptions and challenged our thinking early. Dedicated UX Research then deepened our understanding of player needs.

03 · Key decisions

Fix what matters before changing how it looks

1

Foundations before features

Why
A polished interface wouldn’t fix slow or inaccurate results.
How
Phase one focused on speed, accuracy, data quality and consistency. Bigger ideas from the ideation workshop, like intent-based and mood-based search, were parked for later.
2

Evolve, don’t reinvent

Why
The biggest problems were how search worked, not how it looked.
How
Started with a two-tile layout using existing game tiles, so we could move fast and learn before moving to three tiles.
3

Same foundation, different eyeshadow

Why
Three brands needed one system, but each has its own personality.
How
Shared functionality and patterns, with brand flexibility in type, tone, colour and tile styling, agreed with Brand and Commercial.

One foundation, three brands

“Same foundation. Different eyeshadow.”

How I described the approach to stakeholders

The search experience and core patterns are shared. Typography, tone of voice, colour and tile treatments, like Paddy Power’s green tile borders, stay true to each brand. We’re also working with Data to understand how search behaviour differs by brand, so the shared system can become more relevant to each audience over time.

05 · Bringing people together

Turning uncertainty into a shared direction

In August, I brought Product, Design, the Product Owner and Engineering into the same room to work through refreshed GA4 data, existing research and technical constraints.

The biggest shift was how we framed filtering. Instead of debating whether filters go before or after searching, we looked at how search and filtering could work independently and support each other.

The most useful decisions came from balancing ambition with getting the basics right, and from getting the right people in the same room early.

In phase one, I’m watching whether faster, more accurate results actually lift the launch rate before we commit to three tiles.
