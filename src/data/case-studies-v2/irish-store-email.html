01 · Context

More subscribers, same old email

Email was one of The Irish Store’s most profitable channels. During Covid, the subscriber list grew sharply, but the key numbers didn’t follow. Every customer got the same email, whether they’d just signed up or had been shopping for years, and it wasn’t built for the phones most of them were reading on.

We set targets for 12-month retention, lead conversion, new customers and revenue, plus clearer segmentation between new and existing customers.

02 · What we learned

Most people read on mobile, and new subscribers felt overwhelmed

67%of new subscribers prefer mobile
48%of existing customers prefer mobile
1 in 4competitors had a responsive email

I combined email analytics, help desk tickets, a session with the Customer Service Manager, a review of four competitors and a survey split between new and existing customers.

New subscribers: too many emails, and unsure which promotion was live. Existing customers: happy with the frequency, but wanted more brand and blog content.

Survey takeaway

The UX issues were practical ones: images loading slowly, text too small on mobile, no easy way to copy a promo code, and email-only offers that led to a confusing website journey.

03 · Key decisions

Build a system, not a template

1

Code it by hand

Why
The email platform’s builder (Bronto) wasn’t fit for purpose and couldn’t handle responsive layouts.
How
Separate desktop (600px) and mobile (320px) templates, coded in HTML and CSS from a table-based grid.
2

Modular components

Why
Every campaign needed different content and hierarchy. One fixed template couldn’t do that.
How
A library of Hero, Product and Information components that marketing can combine for any campaign.
3

Segment new and existing customers

Why
The two groups wanted different things: fewer, clearer emails versus more brand content.
How
Agreed priorities with the E-commerce, Digital Marketing and Product managers, then targeted each group separately.

05 · Testing

Every change, A/B tested

Templates were tested in Outlook, Gmail, Yahoo and the native iOS and Android mail apps before going live. Then we A/B tested as we went:

  • Responsive vs non-responsive: the responsive template got a higher click rate.
  • HTML vs image buttons: heatmaps showed more engagement with HTML buttons.
  • Landing page vs homepage: for email-only offers, a dedicated landing page gave the best experience and reduced customer service queries, so we rolled it out for all email-only promotions.

06 · Impact

Fewer emails, better results

  • 12-month retention up 5% and lead conversion up 4.2%, both beating their targets.
  • New customers up 5% and revenue up 5.9% year on year.
  • Segmentation meant customers received fewer, more relevant emails.
  • Average order value dipped slightly, because the business ran more aggressive promotions that year. Other factors also influenced these numbers.

The final result: one Black Friday campaign, built from the new components, on desktop and mobile.

What I took from it

Design flexible systems rather than one-off layouts, test assumptions instead of guessing, and think about the whole journey: the email doesn’t end at the click.

I’d push earlier for a better email builder, instead of working around one that wasn’t fit for purpose. Hand-coding worked, but it made the system harder for others to maintain.
