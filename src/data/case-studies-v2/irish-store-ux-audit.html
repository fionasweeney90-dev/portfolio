The Irish StoreUX Audit & Improvements · E-commerce · Completed

Customers couldn’t find the card option at checkout. Fixing it lifted conversion by 15%.

A research-led audit of a fast-growing e-commerce site, focused on quick, high-impact fixes rather than a full redesign.

The problem

A surge of new customers in 2020 brought a surge of customer service queries, and checkout was losing people.

My role

One of three UX designers. Research, analysis, prototyping and developer handover.

Key decision

Prioritise quick wins with stakeholders, by effort and impact, instead of a full redesign.

The result

All three 10% targets beaten: conversion, checkout abandonment and customer queries.

01 · Context

Growth exposed the friction

During Covid, The Irish Store saw a huge jump in new customers. With that growth, more issues started coming through customer service, and the business needed to fix them without losing the customers it had just gained.

We set three targets: 10% more conversions, 10% less checkout abandonment and 10% fewer customer queries.

Most customers were happy, so the job wasn’t a redesign. It was finding the specific moments where the experience let them down.

02 · What we learned

Checkout looked like PayPal only

Over 66% of customers preferred to pay by card, but the checkout led with PayPal. Card details were only taken at the next step, so many customers assumed card payment wasn’t an option at all.

03 · The audit

Small inconsistencies, adding up

We reviewed the whole site against best practice, from navigation to checkout. None of the issues were big on their own, but together they made the site feel harder to trust and to use.

Navigation & terminology

Menu labels didn’t match page titles, and UK and US terms were mixed, like jumpers and sweaters.

Product pages & sizing

Missing size guides, promotion codes that were easy to miss, and delivery times shown on some products only.

Mobile usability

On smaller phones, much of the content sat off canvas, and tap targets were too small.

04 · Key decisions

Small, considered changes over a full redesign

1

Quick wins first

Why
Customers were broadly happy. We needed results fast, without disrupting what already worked.
How
Stakeholders ranked every audit finding by priority against effort, colour-coded by customer experience area.
2

Put card payment first

Why
Card was the preferred method for most customers, but it looked unavailable.
How
Card moved above PayPal, accepted card logos added, and a message explaining that card details come in the next step.
3

Test search, don’t guess

Why
Search felt clunky, but there was no single obvious fix.
How
We ran a month-long A/B test of different results layouts and let customer behaviour decide.

06 · Impact

Small changes, big difference

  • Conversion up 15.27%, checkout abandonment down 11.8% and customer queries down 10.4%, all beating the 10% targets.
  • Clearer size guides meant fewer sizing questions and fewer returns of the wrong size, though some cases still needed help from staff.

What I took from it

Good research doesn’t always lead to a big redesign. Listening to customers, getting stakeholders around the same table and being disciplined about priorities turned a long list of problems into quick, measurable wins.

I’d set up a way to keep collecting feedback after launch, so the audit became an ongoing habit rather than a one-off project.
